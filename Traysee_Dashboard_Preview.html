Master Appendix & Diagnostic Framework

1. Global Grading Matrix Explained

CEFR (Common European Framework of Reference):

The global standard for describing language ability on a 6-point scale, from A1 (Beginner) to C2 (Mastery). Corporate environments typically require a minimum of B2 (Upper-Intermediate) for fluency.

IELTS (International English Language Testing System):

Scored on a 9-band scale. Widely used for global migration and higher education. A score of 7.0+ indicates a "Good User" capable of handling complex reasoning.

TOEFL (Test of English as a Foreign Language):

Scored from 0 to 120. Primarily utilized by North American institutions. A score of 95+ is generally expected for professional and academic excellence.

GSE (Global Scale of English):

A highly granular scale from 10 to 90. It provides a more precise measurement of progress within a single CEFR band.

2. The 4 Diagnostic Pillars Explained

Acoustic Analysis:

Measures the raw physics of speech. We track Words Per Minute (WPM), Hesitation Density (the ratio of filler words to total words), and Dead Air. A low WPM or high hesitation density signals cognitive stalling and reduces perceived authority.

Lexical Analysis:

Evaluates vocabulary expansion and precision. We track Crutch Words, Vocabulary Gaps, and the use of diminishing adverbs (e.g., "basically", "just") that subconsciously erode executive presence.

Grammatical Analysis:

Monitors structural accuracy. We hunt for False Starts and Tense Confusion, specifically identifying where the syntactic structure of your native language interferes with English delivery.

Coherence Analysis:

Analyzes narrative flow and logical construction. We penalize "Chronological Storytelling" and focus on the BLUF (Bottom-Line Up Front) framework for concise, impactful communication.
